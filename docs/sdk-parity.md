A read-only check of the SDK fork and PdMount against the newest official Proton Drive SDK (M6.0.2, D099), done on
2026-11-18. The fixes are picked by the user in M6.0.4, after M6.0.5's recommendation.

Citations: `cs/…:line` at `cs/v0.33.0`; `sdk/…`, `rs2/…` at upstream 0.5.1 (`base/0.5.1`); patched behaviour by its

**Status:** *same* (the fork and PdMount do what the official client does, or something stricter), *differs*, *missing*,

`cs/README.md:38-47`; Medium when it costs requests, robustness or a visible oddity; Low otherwise.

## Summary

55 findings: 28 same, 16 differ, 7 missing, 4 not applicable. By severity: 4 High, 12 Medium, 9 Low, 30 without
action.

High:
- **W1:** blocks are uploaded without checking that they decrypt; the official SDKs verify every block before upload (P20).
- **R1:** one node that fails to decrypt fails the whole folder listing or event page; the official SDKs return it with
  its errors (P26).
  M6.0.4.3 (V-3, D130) covered part of it: in P13's batch a node under an undecryptable parent is degraded, and the
  poller gives up a page after three non-transient failures; a node that fails by itself in the batch, and listings
  through `NodeBatchLoader`, remain for P26.
- **R2:** one XAttr field that does not decode discards the whole XAttr, and the file then shows its encrypted size and
  reads as EIO (P4 amended).
- **R3:** block sizes written unsorted by a Windows client before 2025 make those files read as EIO; JS sorts them
  (P4 amended).

Nothing found breaks the service rules: identification, endpoints, retries, transfer limits and event cadence are the
official ones or below (§1, §5). The closest is E2: the last share used polls every 30 s until the daemon stops.

How it was done: the official C# and JS sources at the tag, the fork's `pdmount-0.5` against `base/0.5.1`, and
PdMount's adapter, engine and poller, read side by side; the 28 commits after `js/v0.23.0` read one by one. Two areas
(read path with auth, further roots) were searched by research agents and their High and Medium findings checked against
the code before they were taken in. Nothing was run against the real account.

## 8. Q5: the crypto migration in the 28 commits after `js/v0.23.0`

Nothing. None of the 28 commits `js/v0.23.0..cs/v0.33.0` mentions the migration, a new key or encryption version, or a
date: their subjects and bodies name no crypto change beyond dependency updates, and `README.md`, `client/README.md` and
`client/js/README.md` are unchanged, so `cs/README.md:23-28` still says "end of 2026/early 2027", "may shift", with
migration steps to come in the README and changelogs. The changelogs since then (`cs/v0.32.0`, `cs/v0.32.1`, and
`js/v0.23.0`, which had none at the time of the upstream port) list search, albums and node-type names only.

The crypto-related commits:
- `52b42079` upgrades `@protontech/crypto` to 2.2.0; its patch (`cs/config/js/patches/@protontech%2Fcrypto@2.2.0.patch`)
  only switches the import from `openpgp/lightweight` to `openpgp` and adjusts stream types.
- `e3d029d2` fixes a memory leak when decoding armored messages and signatures by raising the C# crypto packages
  (`cs/config/cs/Packages.props`: the Go backend, the default, 0.31.0 → 0.32.1; the Rust backend 0.31.0-rust.1 → 0.40.1).
- `3b8d4428` adds the invitee's acceptance signature check (F3), with the existing signing contexts.
- `05c91159` builds the Kotlin client without the legacy Core; no change to the Drive crypto.

Q5 therefore stays as it is: watch `cs/README.md` and the changelogs (M7).
